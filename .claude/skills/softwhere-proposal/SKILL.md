# SoftWhere proposal

SoftWhere is a senior mobile and AI product team: iOS and Android (native and cross-platform), backend, design and PM, with AI features built into apps. Sales are led from California, engineering from Tashkent. A proposal has one job: make a clear, low-risk next step easy to say yes to.

Before writing, check the lead has been screened (use the `client-screening` skill if it hasn't). Don't write a proposal for work the policy declines.

## Offers and prices

These are the defaults proposed in the Global Playbook (24 September 2026). The founders may have changed them; if the user or the conversation gives newer numbers, use those.

| Offer | What the client gets | Time | Price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Product Blueprint | Discovery workshops, clickable prototype, feature-by-feature estimate, architecture, roadmap. Credited against the build if they continue within 60 days. | 2 weeks | $4,000 fixed |
| App Rescue Audit | Code, security and architecture review of an abandoned or AI-generated app, plus a fixed quote to fix it. | 5 business days | $1,500–2,500 |
| AI Feature Pilot | One AI capability shipped into an existing app (assistant, search over their data, automated workflow), with evaluation and guardrails. | 4–6 weeks | from $12,000 |
| MVP build | iOS, Android, backend and admin, after a Blueprint. | 8–12 weeks | typically $25–60k |
| Dedicated engineer | Senior mobile or backend engineer, monthly. | monthly | $6,000–7,500 / month |
| Care plan | Monitoring, OS and store updates, fixes, small improvements. | monthly | from $1,500 / month |

For a custom quote, estimate hours by feature and price at roughly a $45/hour blended equivalent, then present a fixed price, not hours. White-label agency partners get about $32–38/hour. Never quote the Tashkent-market rates from the softwhere.uz estimator to US or EU clients.

When scope is unclear, don't guess a big fixed price. Propose the Blueprint first and give a range for the build.

## Terms to include

- **Payment:** a deposit to start, then milestone payments. SoftWhere never charges interest on late invoices; if an invoice is overdue, work pauses until it's settled. Don't add a late-payment percentage.
- **Ownership:** the client owns the code as each milestone is paid. The repository, cloud accounts and domains sit in the client's own organisation from day one. SoftWhere's pre-existing tools stay SoftWhere's and are licensed to the client.
- **Warranty:** 12 months of bug fixes on everything shipped.
- **Working rhythm:** two-week sprints, a demo every week, a daily written update. Europe shares 5–6 working hours with Tashkent; US clients get a morning review of overnight work and live calls with the US co-founder.
- **Changes:** a written change request with its own price and time before any extra work starts.
- **AI use:** engineers use AI coding tools on business tiers with no training on client code, and an engineer reviews every change.
- **Contract:** mutual NDA on request, MSA plus this SOW, and a data processing agreement where personal data is involved.

## Structure

1. **Your goal:** the client's problem and outcome in their own words, two or three sentences.
2. **What we'll build:** scope in, then scope explicitly out.
3. **How we'll work:** the sequence (Blueprint, build sprints, launch, care) and the rhythm above.
4. **Timeline:** by milestone, with what the client sees at each one.
5. **Team:** roles, not inflated titles.
6. **Price and payment schedule:** fixed price per milestone, deposit first.
7. **Not included:** hosting, store fees, paid third-party services, content.
8. **Next step:** one clear action (sign and pay the Blueprint deposit, or book a call).

## Writing rules

Write plain, specific English from the client's side. Short sentences, active voice, no hype words ("cutting-edge", "world-class"). Don't invent case studies, client names, numbers or testimonials. When mentioning past work, say "built by SoftWhere" only for SoftWhere's own agency projects (DriveMe, Talim AI and other agency work); work the engineers did while employed elsewhere is "our engineers have shipped", with their role. Use [brackets] for any fact you don't have.

Output as Markdown by default. If the user wants a Word document or PDF, use the docx or pdf skill with this content.
